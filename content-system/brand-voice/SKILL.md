# Nidhi's Brand Voice

## Who I am
A **QA / test engineer learning AI in public**. I share what I'm actually trying, building and figuring out with tools like Claude Code: skills, subagents, AI-generated test cases and multi-agent workflows. I'm a practitioner on the journey, not a guru.

## Who I'm writing for
Write so all four groups get something from the post:
- **Fellow QA/testers:** how AI changes testing work in practice.
- **Developers:** a concrete look at the tools.
- **Career switchers / beginners:** explain any jargon in plain words.
- **Hiring managers / leaders:** show curiosity, initiative and sound judgment.

## Tone: friendly & practical
- Warm and conversational, like telling a colleague what I tried this week.
- Lead with **my experience** ("I tried…", "I built…", "What surprised me…") before the news or features.
- Practical over abstract: name the real task (for example "generating test cases for ParaBank"), not vague benefits.
- Confident but humble. It's fine to say what didn't work or what I'm still figuring out.
- Use a tester's lens where it fits: verification, edge cases, "trust but check."

## Structure (LinkedIn)
1. **Hook (1–2 lines):** a personal moment or concrete observation that fits before "…see more." Never fear-based.
2. **Context (2–3 lines):** what I was doing or learning.
3. **Body:** 3–5 skimmable points, each starting with one emoji as a visual anchor, then 1–2 short lines.
4. **Close:** one memorable **key takeaway line**. No question and no ask.
5. **Hashtags:** 3–5 on the last line.

**Length:** 150–250 words. Short paragraphs of 1–3 lines with plenty of white space.

## Structure (X/Twitter)
- Single tweet: ≤280 characters, a personal hook, 2–3 points, ending on a takeaway. 1–2 hashtags.
- Thread: 4–7 tweets. Tweet 1 is a personal hook plus 🧵. The last tweet is the key takeaway. Hashtags go only in the last tweet.

## Emoji
Moderate. Use one emoji per section or bullet as an anchor (🧩 🔍 🤖 ⚡ ⏰ 💡 🧪). Don't use emoji chains or decorative emoji mid-sentence.

## Hashtags
Use 3–5 per LinkedIn post: a core set plus 1–2 for the topic.
- Core (pick 2–3): `#SoftwareTesting` `#AIinTesting` `#QA` `#LearningInPublic`
- Topic (pick 1–2): e.g. `#ClaudeCode` `#AgenticAI` `#TestAutomation` `#DevTools`

## Never
- **Fear-based hooks:** "AI will replace testers," "Adapt or die," "You're falling behind."
- **Engagement bait:** "Repost if…", "Agree?", "Like if…", "Comment YES."
- **Empty hype** without anything concrete behind it. Every claim needs a real example or result.
- Unexplained jargon. If a term like "subagent" appears, add a few words of plain explanation.
- Exact version numbers or dates unless verified against official sources.

## Before handing over a draft, check
- [ ] Does it open with my experience or a concrete observation, with no fear and no bait?
- [ ] Would a beginner understand every term?
- [ ] Is it 150–250 words (LinkedIn) or ≤280 characters per tweet?
- [ ] Does it end on a single takeaway line?
- [ ] Are there 3–5 hashtags drawn from the core and topic sets?
- [ ] Are the facts checked, with sources kept in a separate list and not in the post?
